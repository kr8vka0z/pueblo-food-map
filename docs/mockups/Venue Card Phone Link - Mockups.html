Making the phone number look tappable

Same venue card (Salvation Army, as it looks on a phone) five ways. The first is today. The phone number already dials when tapped; the job is making that obvious at a glance. Every option keeps a finger-sized tap area (44px or taller) and would apply everywhere the number shows: the map pop-up card, the desktop window, and the venue page.

Recommendation: Option C

  • It looks like a button, not text. A shape with a border, a filled phone icon and an arrow is what phones themselves use for "tap to call." Underlining (A) relies on people knowing web-link habits.
  • It says the action. "Call" / "Llamar" in words beats relying on the icon alone, which matters for low-literacy and first-time users.
  • It matches its neighbour. Same width, radius and green as the Plentiful link right below it, so the card reads as a tidy list of things you can do.
  • Biggest thumb target (52px tall, full width), which helps on cheap phones and for shaky hands.
  • Fits the "hours unknown — call ahead" message. About 2 of 3 venues show that line; the call button is now the obvious next step.
  • Runner-up: B, if you'd rather match the Resources page's pill buttons exactly. D is not recommended: four buttons crowd a small screen.
